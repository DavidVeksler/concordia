## What you see

Noordung is the second rotor, paired with Tsiolkovsky and turning the same way, and it is the station's capital quarter. Its city was drawn by the same generator as [Arbor Quarter](/places/arbor-quarter/) from its own seed, then weighted toward dense urban cores and civic land. The streets are paved and stately, with green squares between the towers.

{% shot "sc-rotor2-civic-street" "A paved civic street in Noordung with a stately frontage on one side and a green square ahead." %}

Three halls stand out. Each has a name board over its portico, set in capitals, so a resident walking past always knows where they are. The Exchange and the Court are about 800 m apart, on neighbouring streets. Charter Hall stands five to six kilometres around the ring from them, in another of Noordung's core districts.

## The Exchange

A domed market hall behind a portico, with a colonnade on its own plaza. Energy and compute for the whole station are priced and traded here. Those two things are what Concordia sells: solar platforms with computers behind them, whose answers travel to Earth as light. The [economy page](/station/economy/#what-concordia-sells) has the numbers and the reason the watts leave as bits.

## Charter Hall

A vaulted hall behind a portico, with two gate towers on its lawn. It is the seat of the Concordia Charter Authority, the body that holds the station's charter. Why the station has a charter at all, and who it answers to, is set out under [Charter and law](/station/economy/#charter-and-law).

## The Court

A clock-roofed hall with a columned portico and pediment, and a monument column on its plaza. The arbitration courts sit here, in the same quarter as the Exchange. They stand a short walk from the market because deals struck there are settled here. The law they work within is set out under [Charter and law](/station/economy/#charter-and-law): markets inside, on top of a strict physical constitution.

## Why a capital rotor

The station puts its money in one rotor and its estates in another. Noordung carries the Exchange, the Charter Hall and the Court, and O'Neill (Rotor 4) holds the large lakeside plots, because on a habitat the scarcest luxury is open ground under the sky. [Who lives here](/station/economy/#who-lives-here) covers the owners, the dividend every resident receives and why 400,000 people live on a station that needs only hundreds to run it.
