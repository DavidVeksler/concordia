## The hull

The outer shell is a deep open truss with removable shielding cassettes on its surface. It is not the pressure vessel: the habitat's air is held hundreds of metres inward, so a punctured panel does not open a home to space. The cassettes are compacted regolith and slag in 32 m tiles, 609 around the ring, between ribs every 128 m along the axis and every 96 m around it.

{% shot "pl-exterior-hull" "Close on the hull: shielding cassettes, repair seams, a maintenance rail and the service robot at an open slot." %}

The skin looks patched because it is. Cassettes come in four types, fitted over the years and replaced as they wear, so some panels are years older than others. Rails run along the hull for crawlers that swap them. The shielding is broken in two places: over the open industrial bay, and from the shipyard to the port, where an open cage lets finished ships leave. See [Radiation and debris](/science/radiation-and-debris/) for how the stack works.

## Radiators, dishes and arrays

Nearly all of the station's electricity and body heat ends up as heat, and in space it can only leave as light. Eight radiator banks stand off the hull on pylons, 248 panels of 400 by 200 m in all, running at 380 to 500 K. Each bank turns slowly to keep its edge to the Sun. Four dish stations carry 170 m reflectors and optical terminals, and one backup PV collector stands on a 1,100 m mast.

{% shot "pl-exterior-radiators" "A bank of radiator panels on pylons and a spine, with the habitat for scale." %}

The launch track for passenger craft runs 10 km along the hull, and two cargo mass drivers 15 to 16 km long run on opposite sides. See [Power and heat](/science/power-and-heat/) for the radiator physics and [Getting there](/science/getting-there/) for the track.

## Defence and satellites

### Defence

Four sites guard the station: two forward and two aft. Radar fences, an 85 m tracking dish, survey telescopes and lidar masts watch for debris, and heavy and rapid interceptors, point-defence guns and capture-net launchers answer it. Layered detection and interception cover each end, and [radiation and debris](/science/radiation-and-debris/) explains where shielding stops and interception starts. Directed energy is left out. Cargo from the Moon and the asteroids is caught tens or hundreds of kilometres away and towed in, never aimed at the habitat.

{% shot "pl-exterior-defence" "A forward defence battery with armoured housing, reload magazine, interceptor cells and a blast wall." %}

### Satellites

Seven craft fly free of the hull. P1 is a tethered solar farm 7 km aft. P2 and P3 are formation-flying power platforms that send power by a microwave link. V1 and V2 are volatiles depots. R1 is the Earth relay and S1 is the forward sentinel. The overlay shows them: {% explore viewpoint="complete-habitat" overlay="satellites" label="Satellites overlay" %}

{% deeper "Hull stack and fleet" %}
The layers from outside in: sacrificial impact layer, standoff vacuum, regolith or slag cassette layer, structural shell, service vacuum, hydrogen-rich local shielding, then the rotating pressure vessel. Thickness varies with what lies behind it. For scale, 1 m of regolith at 1.6 t/m³ over the whole 470 km² is about 750 million tonnes.

Radiator banks: the top and bottom banks are fields of 2,200 × 1,000 m on 1,100 m stand-offs, and the side banks are 3,100 × 1,500 m on 1,600 m. At 450 K a single-sided radiator rejects about 2.1 kW/m².

P1 is a tethered farm at x = −17,500 m. Power reaches the station through four aft masts; see [Aft works](/places/aft-works/). Full buildout at about 40 GW needs roughly 28 platforms of 4.2 km².

The defence sites are DEF-F1 and F2 at x = 9,728 m and DEF-A1 and A2 at about x = −9,472 m. Heavy interceptors are 36 m long and about 120 t.
